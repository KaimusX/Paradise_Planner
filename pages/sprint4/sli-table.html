The user hurt most by a failure

The traveler who reaches the day view and cannot tell whether they are over budget. If cost is not visible, the core promise of the charter (planning a trip more efficiently than doing it manually) collapses, and the user is back to a spreadsheet.

Two ways we measure it

SLI 1 and SLI 2 compared
SLI 1: total is shown and correctSLI 2: users find the total
DefinitionProportion of day-view loads where the running trip total is displayed and matches the sum of listed activity costs.Proportion of test users who locate the running total without scrolling or asking for help.
MeasurementAutomated check on each prototype build; result logged per load. Owner: Joy.Moderated usability test, 5 classmates, one task per session. Owner: Joy, with one teammate observing.
SLO100% of loads in the prototype sprint, checked on every build.At least 4 of 5 users locate the running total without scrolling or asking for help, during the wireframe-to-prototype sprint.
Rule checkRatio of good events to total events; measured from the user's side; has a time window per build; specific and unambiguous.Same ratio form; user-centric; scoped to one sprint; states the population (5 users); testable as written.
Who would noticeThe traveler who spots a wrong total and stops trusting the budget feature.The traveler who gives up and opens a spreadsheet instead.

Rule check

Both indicators follow the five rules: measured from the user's perspective, expressed as a ratio of good to total events, specific, bounded by a time window, and observable with tools the team actually has.

SLI 2 is the weaker of the two only because five test users is a small population. The SLO is phrased as “4 of 5” rather than a percentage that would imply more precision than the sample supports.

Dashboard note

Because this table feeds the Sprint 4 dashboard, both rows carry a named owner and a real cadence: SLI 1 on every build, SLI 2 once per sprint during usability testing.
