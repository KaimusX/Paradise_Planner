Sprint 1 — Team 11

Business Strategy

Paradise Planner is designed to make travel planning easier, faster, and more financially predictable by combining itinerary organization with budget awareness in one experience.

Our strategy connects a low-cost travel planning product with affiliate-based revenue while helping travelers make better-informed trip decisions.

01 Strategy

The strategy for our travel planner app is to help everyday travelers plan their next vacation with confidence by combining trip itinerary building with real-time budget tracking, so users know exactly what they can afford before they book.

Designed for frequent leisure travelers who want more financial control over their trips, the app is offered at $1.99, making it accessible with no recurring fees or subscription fatigue.

Beyond the initial purchase, the app generates ongoing revenue through affiliate partnerships with booking platforms like Booking.com and Expedia, earning a small commission whenever users book flights, hotels, or activities directly through the app. This model keeps the app low-cost and appealing to download while creating a sustainable revenue stream tied to the very trips users are already planning.

02 Objective

The main business objective of Paradise Planner is to make trip planning easier, faster, and more organized for users who are trying to balance destination choices with a limited budget. The product should help users answer these five questions:

  • Where should I go?
  • Where should I stay?
  • How should I travel?
  • What activities should I include?
  • Does the trip fit my expected budget?

Success Criterion

A successful result is a user being able to create and organize a complete trip more efficiently with Paradise Planner than by manually searching multiple websites and tracking the information separately.

03 Justification

The problem

Planning information is spread across multiple tools.

  • Flights
  • Hotels
  • Transportation
  • Activities
  • Budget
→

Paradise Planner

Important planning information brought together so users can see how their choices affect the overall trip.

Travel planning is often fragmented because travelers must use different websites or applications for flights, hotels, activities, transportation, and budgeting. This creates additional work because the traveler must compare information from several sources and then manually organize the results.

Our research indicates that cost and available activities or events are major factors when people make travel decisions. Paradise Planner addresses this problem by bringing the most important planning information together and allowing users to see how different choices affect their overall trip.

The project is justified because it addresses a common and practical problem: travelers need a simpler way to understand the cost, timing, and activities involved in a trip before making final decisions. By reducing the number of separate tools required, Paradise Planner can make the planning process easier to understand and more efficient.

04 Scope

Paradise Planner focuses on helping users organize trips and make major travel decisions.

In Scope

  • Destinations

    Choosing and comparing destinations.

  • Lodging

    Reviewing hotel or lodging options.

  • Transportation

    Comparing flights or other transportation options.

  • Expense estimates

    Estimating major expenses such as lodging, flights, gas, food, and activities.

  • Activities & events

    Finding relevant activities and events at the destination.

  • Daily itinerary

    Organizing activities, costs, and times into a day-by-day itinerary.

  • Total trip cost

    Viewing an estimated total trip cost.

  • External service links

    Redirecting users to external travel providers when a booking or purchase needs to be completed outside the application.

Scope Boundary

The project does not attempt to become a complete booking platform or provide information about every event occurring at every destination. Paradise Planner is primarily a planning and decision-support tool. Trips will also be planned within approximately 12 months.

05 Where the Chain Breaks and Why

Greatest risk

External Data Dependency

Paradise Planner depends on external sources for hotel prices, flights, events, and other travel information.

If that data becomes unavailable, outdated, incomplete, or incompatible, users may not receive enough useful information to make effective travel decisions.

This is the greatest risk between the application's features and the expected business outcome.

Booking handoff

External Provider Experience

Paradise Planner can help users compare and organize their choices, but it cannot fully control what happens once a user is redirected to an external provider to complete a booking.

Project focus

Scope Creep

Trying to support every event, every travel service, or complete booking functionality could make the application too complicated and move the project away from its main purpose.

Protecting the Strategy

The team should protect the strategy-to-project chain by keeping development focused on features that directly support the main objective:

  • Cost visibility
  • Destination decisions
  • Transportation
  • Lodging
  • Activities
  • Simple day-by-day trip organization
